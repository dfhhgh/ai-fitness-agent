Scientific Verification Report1. Overall AssessmentThe scientific and policy foundation of the Calorie Target Calculator Policy Specification (Phase 2.2.4, Policy ID: calorie-v1, Revision Rev1.1) is acceptable with minor wording and reference corrections.The software core architecture defines a stateless, pure function that executes a deterministic linear transformation:$$\text{Target Calories} = TDEE + \text{Calorie Adjustment}$$where Total Daily Energy Expenditure ($TDEE$) is supplied by an upstream component ($TDEECalculator$) and modified by a fixed adjustment parameter determined by $GoalType$. This architectural isolation enforces a clean operational separation between basic deterministic business logic and non-deterministic or contextual generative models.The primary requirement prior to final code execution is to refine specific scientific claims in Section 19 ("Scientific Basis vs. Engineering Decisions") and align terminology across the policy text. The specification correctly categorizes static energy modifications ($\pm 500\text{ kcal/day}$) and the low-calorie review threshold ($1,200\text{ kcal/day}$) as baseline software engineering decisions ($V1\text{ Engineering Policy}$) rather than immutable physiological laws. However, certain citations and contextual statements—specifically the direct attribution of exact caloric ranges to the International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) and the historical representation of Wishnofsky's rule—require precise wording corrections to maintain absolute scientific rigour.2. Applying a rigid numerical floor (clamping) across all user profiles would introduce false clinical assumptions into a deterministic calculation node. A small, sedentary elderly female may maintain energy balance at a total expenditure near $1,200\text{ kcal/day}$, whereas a tall, active young adult restricted to $1,200\text{ kcal/day}$ would experience a severe hypocaloric deficit risking rapid lean tissue catabolism and micronutrient insufficiency.Because the calorie-v1 calculator is intentionally isolated from biological demographic context ($age, gender, height, weight$), it lacks the domain data required to make contextual clinical assessments. Defining $1,200\text{ kcal/day}$ as an uncontextualized software flag (LOW_CALORIE_REVIEW_REQUIRED) preserves mathematical transparency while alerting downstream safety and clinical review layers to evaluate overall diet adequacy.Precise Replacement Wording for Section 15Section 15 of the policy specification must be updated with the following explicit statement:15. Similarly, an energy surplus of $500\text{ kcal/day}$ provides sufficient available energy to support resistance-training adaptations and body mass accretion.Assigning an identical positive adjustment ($+500\text{ kcal/day}$) to both weight_gain and muscle_gain is an acceptable simplification for initial software deployment. However, scientific documentation must note that muscle hypertrophy depends primarily on mechanical resistance stimulus and adequate protein intake ($1.6\text{–}2.2\text{ g/kg/day}$), whereas an unmanaged surplus of $+500\text{ kcal/day}$ in untrained individuals will result in excess adipose tissue storage alongside lean mass accrual.Precise Replacement Wording for Section 11Section 11 should be updated with the following text:11. Goal Mapping (V1 Operational Baseline)To enforce absolute functional determinism in version 1 (calorie-v1), the calculator translates discrete goal selections into fixed energy adjustments:maintenance: $0\text{ kcal/day}$weight_loss: $-500\text{ kcal/day}$weight_gain: $+500\text{ kcal/day}$muscle_gain: $+500\text{ kcal/day}$These fixed offsets represent operational baseline parameters for initial software execution and are not general physiological laws. The application of $+500\text{ kcal/day}$ for muscle_gain assumes that dietary protein distribution and structured resistance exercise are managed by separate domain components.5. >3500 RuleThe decision in Revision Rev1.1 to remove the high-calorie review flag (HIGH_CALORIE_REVIEW_REQUIRED for targets exceeding $3,500\text{ kcal/day}$) is scientifically accurate and architecturally required.Daily energy expenditure varies widely across populations. In athletic cohorts, large individuals, or individuals engaging in high-volume endurance or strength training, basal metabolic demands combined with high physical activity factors routinely yield total energy expenditures exceeding $3,500\text{ kcal/day}$.Triggering an uncontextualized high-calorie safety flag at $3,500\text{ kcal/day}$ within a pure mathematical core introduces false-positive system alerts for athletic user profiles. Evaluating whether a high caloric target is excessive or unsafe requires analyzing total daily intake relative to measured expenditure ($TDEE$), body mass, training volume, and clinical goals. Consequently, high-calorie safety checks belong exclusively in upper contextual review layers.6.
